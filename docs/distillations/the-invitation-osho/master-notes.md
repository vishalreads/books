## Executive Architectural Overview: The Call to the Living Well in Pune Two

Delivered in late summer 1987 in the intimate, charged atmosphere of Chuang Tzu Auditorium, *The Invitation* marks one of the most mature, crystalline, and powerful discourse series of Osho’s final Pune Two era. Having survived the catastrophic collapse of Rajneeshpuram in Oregon, twelve days of shackled detention in US federal penitentiaries, the covert administration of thallium poisoning in Oklahoma, and a harrowing world tour in which twenty-one democratic nations slammed their borders against him, Osho had returned home to 17 Koregaon Park in Pune.

The tone of *The Invitation* is distinct from all earlier periods:
- The flamboyant, external theatrics of the 1970s had been stripped away.
- The crowd had matured; the seekers who gathered in Chuang Tzu Auditorium were seasoned disciples who had remained loyal through state persecution and media storms.
- Osho was visibly in physical pain, his body ravaged by the destruction of his cardiovascular and immune systems, yet his spiritual radiance, intellectual brilliance, and razor-sharp humor burned with unprecedented luminosity.

The discourse series opens with one of the most celebrated existential questions in modern spiritual history, posed by his disciple Maneesha:
> **"Beloved Osho, can you say who you are?"**

Osho’s response defines the foundational architecture of the entire work:
> *"Maneesha, I am an invitation for all those who are seeking, searching, and have a deep longing in their hearts to find their home.  
> I am an answer to the question that everybody is, but cannot formulate—a question that is more a quest than a question, more a thirst than a verbal, mental inquiry; a thirst that one feels in every cell and fiber of his being, but has no way to bring to words and ask.  
> When I say I am the answer, I don't mean that I can give you the answer... yes, if you are ready, you can take it. I am just like a well, ready for you to throw your bucket and draw the water for yourself. I have it, but I cannot reach to you without your efforts. Only you can reach to me."*

Across thirty comprehensive discourses, Osho expounds this metaphor of **The Well and The Bucket**, guiding the seeker to discard the false burdens of ancestral guilt, dissolve the synthetic personality, master the pristine art of witnessing (*Sakshi*), and step boldly into the radical archetype of **Zorba the Buddha**.

## Unit 1: The Invitation: The Living Well and the Architecture of Awakening

### 1.1 The Nature of the Living Well
Osho’s definition of his role completely dismantles traditional concepts of religious authority, messiahship, and priesthood:
- A priest or guru demands belief, obedience, money, and subservience:
  - *"Believe what I tell you, follow my commandments, submit your will to mine, and I will save your soul."*
- Osho rejects this entirely:
  - He is not a savior; he has no interest in saving anyone.
  - He is simply a **living well of water**:
  - The well does not go running after thirsty people in the village shouting, *"Drink from me!"*
  - The well simply sits quietly in its own depths, full of cool, sweet, subterranean water.
  - If a traveler is truly thirsty, the traveler must walk to the well, lower their own rope and bucket, and pull the water up with their own strength.
  - The well gives freely, unconditionally, with zero demands and zero expectations.

### 1.2 The Transition to Pune Two
In *The Invitation*, Osho explains the shift in his public presence:
- In the 1970s, he had to use aggressive devices, controversial statements on sex and politics, and theatrical ashram setups to wake up a sleeping world and attract seekers from across the globe.
- Now, that preliminary work was finished:
  - The seekers who had arrived in Pune Two were not curious tourists.
  - They were ready for the **pure, unadulterated essence of meditation**.
  - Speaking was no longer an intellectual argument; it was an energetic cradle designed to induce deep, wordless communal silence.

## Unit 2: The Anatomy of Seeking: From Restless Questioning to the Thirst of the Soul

### 2.1 The Two Modes of Inquiry
In discourses two and three, Osho distinguishes between two radically different human states:
1. **The Questioning Mind (The Intellect):**
   - Curious, analytical, skeptical, and superficial.
   - It collects words, concepts, theories, and book-knowledge.
   - You can answer a thousand questions of the intellect, and tomorrow it will invent a thousand more! The intellect is a machine that manufactures doubt and debate for its own survival.
2. **The Thirst of the Soul (The Existential Quest):**
   - Born not in the brain, but in the depths of the heart and the gut.
   - It is the agonizing realization that despite having food, clothes, a home, money, and social status, your life feels empty, mechanical, and meaningless.
   - It is the cry of a drowning person gasping for air.
   - For this thirst, words, dogmas, and philosophical systems are useless; only the **living water of direct conscious realization** can quench it.

### 2.2 The Inversion of the Seeker and the Sought
Osho reveals the ultimate Zen paradox of spiritual seeking:
- As long as you are seeking something "out there"—an external God in heaven, an enlightened master in India, a mystical experience in the future—you are looking in the wrong direction!
- The external search keeps the ego alive.
- The breakthrough occurs when the seeker suddenly stops running, turns around, and looks at **the one who is seeking**:
  > *"The treasure you are running after across the world is seated right in the heart of the runner! You are what you are looking for. The seeker is the sought!"*

## Unit 3: The Death of the Past: Dropping the Burdens of Guilt, Sin, and Ancestral Karma

### 3.1 The Weapon of Manufactured Guilt
In multiple discourses throughout *The Invitation*, Osho delivers a scathing, forensic critique of historical religions:
- How have organized priesthoods and churches controlled billions of human beings for thousands of years?
  - Through the deliberate, systematic injection of **guilt**.
  - From infancy, children are taught that they are born in "sin", that their natural biological desires are evil, that they must constantly repent, confess, and feel unworthy.
- **The Psychology of the Crippled Soul:**
  - A person burdened by guilt cannot look at themselves with dignity.
  - They feel internally filthy, broken, and fearful of divine punishment.
  - A guilty person is docile, obedient, and completely dependent on priests, temples, and churches to mediate between them and God.

### 3.2 The Myth of Ancestral Karma
Osho attacks the fatalistic misuse of the doctrine of Karma in Indian society:
- Orthodox priests tell the poor, the sick, and the oppressed: *"You are suffering because of sins you committed in your past lives! Accept your misery quietly and serve the upper castes, so you may have a better rebirth in your next life."*
- Osho denounces this as an ideological conspiracy designed to prevent social revolution and justify inequality.
- He proclaims the **absolute purity of the present moment**:
  - The past is dead! It has no independent existence; it exists only as a mental memory trace.
  - The moment you drop your identification with your memory, the entire mountain of ancestral karma vanishes like a nightmare upon waking!
  - In this very second, you are as pure, innocent, and unburdened as a newborn babe.

## Unit 4: The Courage of Solitude: Moving Beyond Herd Conformity to Sacred Aloneness

### 4.1 The Psychology of the Herd
Why is human society so terrified of the individual?
- A herd animal is safe, predictable, and obedient.
- In a crowd, you do not have to think, take responsibility, or make difficult choices; the crowd thinks for you, decides for you, and protects you.
- But the price of belonging to the herd is **the surrender of your soul**:
  - You must wear the herd’s uniform, repeat the herd’s slogans, hate the herd’s enemies, and swallow the herd’s lies.
- The moment an authentic individual emerges—someone who thinks for themselves, who refuses to bow to dogmas, who lives by their own light—the herd turns violent:
  - They poisoned Socrates; they crucified Jesus; they assassinated Mansoor; they tried to destroy Osho.
  - Why? Because the sovereign individual exposes the cowardice and falsity of the herd!

### 4.2 Loneliness vs. Aloneness (*Kaivalya*)
Osho clarifies the profound existential difference between two words that sound similar but represent opposite dimensions of consciousness:
- **Loneliness is negative:**
  - It is the painful, aching feeling that someone else is missing.
  - It is a state of inner poverty, starvation, and desperation.
  - The lonely person seeks distraction: television, smartphones, alcohol, parties, or toxic relationships just to avoid being alone with themselves.
- **Aloneness is positive:**
  - It is the blissful realization that **you are complete within yourself!**
  - It is not that someone is missing; it is that YOUR OWN PRESENCE has been discovered.
  - In sacred aloneness (*Kaivalya*), you are an overflowing fountain of peace, silence, and joy.
  - When an alone person loves, they love not as a beggar asking for crumbs, but as an emperor showering gifts from boundless inner wealth.

## Unit 5: The Alchemy of Witnessing: Turning the Mind into a Mirror (*Sakshi*)

### 5.1 The Technology of Non-Judgmental Observation
Throughout *The Invitation*, Osho returns repeatedly to the supreme, universal key of all meditation: **Witnessing (*Sakshi-Bhava*)**:
- What is the mind?
  - The mind is a turbulent river of thoughts, memories, desires, arguments, fears, and daydreams.
  - For thousands of years, ascetic teachers told seekers to "control the mind", "stop the thoughts", or "fight the desires".
- Osho demonstrates that fighting the mind is an absurdity:
  - Who is fighting? The mind is fighting the mind!
  - It is like one hand wrestling the other hand; it produces only exhaustion, frustration, and madness.
- Osho’s scientific alternative: **Be the Mirror**:
  > *"When an ugly person stands before a mirror, does the mirror say, 'Go away, you are ugly'? When a beautiful person stands before a mirror, does the mirror try to hold onto their reflection? The mirror simply reflects! It has no choice, no judgment, no attachment, no repulsion.  
  > Treat your mind like a mirror: when anger arises, simply watch the anger; when sadness arrives, watch the sadness; when peace descends, watch the peace. Do not say, 'This is good' or 'This is bad.' Just watch! The moment you become a pure mirror, the energy that was feeding the thoughts is withdrawn, and the storm disappears into boundless silence."*

### 5.2 The Space Between Thoughts
When witnessing is practiced without strain:
- The speed of the thought-river slows down.
- Gaps begin to appear between thoughts:
  - Thought A passes... and there is an interval of pure silence before Thought B arrives!
  - In that interval, there is no language, no identity, and no ego.
  - In that interval, **You are God! You are the cosmos! You are No-Mind!**

## Unit 6: Beyond the Mask of Personality: Dissolving the Egoic False Self

### 6.1 The Drama of Persona
Osho dissects the structural architecture of human identity:
- The word "person" comes from the Greek theatrical term *persona*, meaning the papier-mâché mask worn by actors in ancient drama.
- From the moment you are born, society manufactures a mask for you:
  - Your name, your religion, your caste, your nationality, your academic degrees, your reputation.
  - You become so accustomed to wearing the mask that you completely forget that **there is a living human face behind the mask!**
- The ego is nothing but this synthetic, collective mask.
- It lives on external validation: praise, promotions, awards, and social status.
- If society withdraws its approval, the ego collapses in terror.

### 6.2 The Original Face (*Honrai no Memmoku*)
Osho revives the classic Zen inquiry: *"What was your original face before your parents were born?"*
- Your original face is **pure consciousness itself**:
  - It has no name, no nationality, no bank account, no race, and no religion.
  - It cannot be harmed by criticism and cannot be inflated by praise.
  - It is untouched by birth, disease, aging, and physical death.
  - To accept Osho’s invitation is to take off the heavy, suffocating mask and breathe freely in your own uncreated original face.

## Unit 7: The Mystery of Love and Freedom: Non-Possessive Relating in the Modern World

### 7.1 Deconstructing Marriage and Ownership
In several passionate discourses in *The Invitation*, Osho addresses disciples struggling with romantic entanglements:
- Conventional marriage is fundamentally an **economic and property contract disguised as love**:
  - It converts two free, living souls into mutual jailers.
  - It creates jealousy, suspicion, continuous domestic warfare, and emotional suffocation.
- Why does possessiveness destroy love?
  - Because love is a living flower; if you pluck it and lock it in a steel safe out of fear that someone will steal it, the flower withers and turns to dust.
  - Love can survive only in the open atmosphere of absolute freedom.

### 7.2 The Art of Relating
Osho proposes replacing the static noun "relationship" with the dynamic verb **"relating"**:
- Do not make a permanent institution out of love.
- Meet each day anew as two independent travelers sharing a path through the forest:
  - Today you walk together hand in hand; celebrate the joy of companionship!
  - Tomorrow the paths may diverge; bow to each other with gratitude for the shared journey, and walk on without bitterness, guilt, or legal lawsuits.
- Love that is born of freedom enriches both partners and becomes a stepping stone to divine cosmic love.

## Unit 8: The Cosmic Laugh: Smashing Spiritual Pomposity and Moral Seriousness

### 8.1 The Infection of Religious Seriousness
Throughout *The Invitation*, Osho punctuates his deepest philosophical dissections with hilarious, irreverent jokes. He explains why humor is a cornerstone of his teaching:
- For thousands of years, holy men, bishops, and swamis have looked as gloomy and somber as undertakers at a funeral:
  - They believe that looking miserable is a sign of piety and holiness.
  - Osho declares that **seriousness is a psychological sickness**:
  - It is the ultimate manifestation of the spiritual ego, which takes itself with deadly, pompous importance!

### 8.2 The Zen of Laughter
What happens when you laugh wholeheartedly at a brilliant joke?
- For two or three seconds, the calculating, argumentative mind stops dead in its tracks!
- You cannot think and laugh at the same time.
- Laughter bypasses the logical left brain, plunges you into the visceral body, and floods your cells with radiant endorphins and vitality.
- Osho declares:
  > *"If you cannot laugh, you cannot meditate! Laughter is the fragrance of the flower of enlightenment. A sage who cannot laugh is merely a corpse that has forgotten to fall down!"*

## Unit 9: The Master as a Presence: Silent Energetic Resonance and the Gateless Gate

### 9.1 The Mystery of Pune Two Satsang
Osho reveals the esoteric mechanism of the evening meetings in Chuang Tzu Auditorium:
- Why does an enlightened master continue to speak thirty discourses in a single month when he has already said everything thousands of times?
  - Osho explains:
  > *"I speak only to create silence! My words are like musical pauses between notes. While you are listening to me, your mind is engaged, fascinated, and waiting for the next word. And in between my words, when I pause... suddenly you fall into the abyss of absolute silence! My words are a trick to lure your noisy mind away so that my silence can touch your heart."*

### 9.2 The Gateless Gate (*Wumenguan*)
Osho draws upon the classic Zen paradox of the **Gateless Gate**:
- The gate to liberation is called "gateless" because:
  - There is no wall, no barrier, and no lock separating you from reality!
  - The door has been wide open for eternity; you were simply looking away in the direction of your desires.
  - The moment you turn around and open your eyes, you realize you have been inside the temple all along.

## Unit 10: The New Dawn: Awakening as Zorba the Buddha and Celebrating the Miracle of Being

### 10.1 The Manifesto of the Golden Future
In the culminating discourses of *The Invitation*, Osho casts his vision toward the 21st century:
- Human civilization stands at a terrifying historical crossroads:
  - On one side lies thermonuclear annihilation, ecological devastation, and artificial intelligence enslaved by greedy politicians.
  - On the other side lies **The New Dawn**: the emergence of an unconditioned, enlightened human consciousness.
- This new human being is **Zorba the Buddha**:
  - Grounded in the earth, loving the body, celebrating science, art, technology, food, music, and love.
  - Yet rooted in the sky: resting in silent witnessing, non-attachment, compassion, and eternal freedom.

### 10.2 Accepting the Invitation
Osho concludes the 30-discourse series with a poignant, tender appeal to every seeker present:
> *"The well is open; the water is sweet, cool, and inexhaustible. Do not stand on the bank arguing about the chemistry of water; do not write theological theses about the shape of the well! Lower your bucket. Drink the water. Quench your ancient thirst. This is my invitation: Come home to yourself, rejoice in the miracle of existence, and sing your Hallelujah to the stars!"*

## Appendix B: Comprehensive Glossary of Terms in *The Invitation*

- **The Living Well**: Osho’s primary metaphor for the enlightened master: passive, non-aggressive, brimming with living truth, requiring the seeker to lower their own bucket.
- **Sakshi (साक्षी)**: The detached witness; the pure observational faculty of consciousness that mirrors bodily and mental phenomena without judgment or clinging.
- **Kaivalya (कैवल्य)**: Luminous, self-sufficient aloneness; the state of being complete within oneself without needing social validation.
- **Persona (मुखौटा)**: The synthetic social mask of name, status, and morality constructed by society to replace authentic individuality.
- **Original Face (मूल चेहरा / Honrai no Memmoku)**: Zen concept of the unconditioned, primordial consciousness existing prior to physical birth and social programming.
- **Gateless Gate (Wumenguan / निद्वार द्वार)**: The Zen paradox that there is no physical or metaphysical barrier separating the seeker from enlightenment; realization is immediate upon awakening.
- **Zorba the Buddha**: The integrated, holistic human archetype synthesizing worldly, sensual celebration with transcendent, silent enlightenment.

## Appendix C: Seven Core Clinical Dialogues from *The Invitation*

During the thirty discourses in Chuang Tzu Auditorium, disciples submitted intimate written inquiries addressing acute personal crises. Osho's surgical responses illustrate the operational psychology of *The Invitation*:

### 1. On the Terror of Inner Madness
> **Questioner:** *"Beloved Osho, whenever I sit in deep silence and the thoughts begin to stop, I feel an overwhelming terror that I am going to lose my mind and go completely insane. What should I do?"*
>
> **Osho:** *"Do you know what 'insanity' really is? Insanity is simply the breakdown of your social persona! 
> 
> Society has fitted you with a tiny, tight cognitive straitjacket called 'normality'. In normality, you are predictable, polite, and miserable. When you meditate, the straitjacket begins to crack. The vast, unfathomable wildness of existence begins to flood in! Naturally, the frightened ego cries out: 'I am going crazy!'
> 
> Rejoice! Go crazy! It is far better to be mad in the bliss of the divine than to be 'sane' in the graveyard of social conformity. Don't fight the madness; jump into the abyss. You will discover that on the other side of your so-called insanity lies the supreme sanity of the Buddha!"*

### 2. On Heartbreak, Betrayal, and Residual Hatred
> **Questioner:** *"My partner has left me after seven years together. I alternate between sobbing with grief and burning with rage. I feel betrayed and discarded."*
>
> **Osho:** *"Nobody has betrayed you. You betrayed yourself the moment you believed that tomorrow was guaranteed!
>
> Love is a wild flower; it grows by the side of the road without an invitation, and it withers when its season is done. You cannot force a flower to bloom forever. When your partner loved you, it was true; now the river has changed its course, and that too is true!
> 
> If you love the person, be grateful for the seven beautiful years of fragrance they brought into your life. Why turn past nectar into present poison? Thank them, bless them, and release them. Use this wound as a sacred doorway: turn your gaze inward to find the lover inside yourself who will never leave you."*

### 3. On Professional Burnout and Corporate Exhaustion
> **Questioner:** *"I work twelve hours a day in a high-stress corporate job in Frankfurt. I earn an enormous salary, but my life feels hollow. Yet I have financial commitments and cannot simply abandon my responsibilities."*
>
> **Osho:** *"You do not have to abandon your job; you have to abandon your IDENTIFICATION with the job!
> 
> In the office, be an actor on a stage. If an actor plays the role of a king on stage, does he think he is actually a king when he goes home to dinner? No! He knows it was just a drama.
> 
> Play the corporate role with total technical competence, but never bring the corporation into your heart. Do not let your bank balance become your soul balance. When the clock strikes five, drop the role completely. Walk in the park, listen to the evening birds, sit with yourself in silence. You are not a machine of production; you are a living song of the cosmos."*

### 4. On Chronic Irritation with Elderly Parents
> **Questioner:** *"Whenever I visit my parents, within ten minutes my mother's nagging and my father's criticism trigger my childhood rage. How can I maintain my meditative center around them?"*
>
> **Osho:** *"If you want to know how enlightened you really are, spend a weekend with your parents! That is the ultimate litmus test!
> 
> Why do your parents provoke you? Because they push the exact psychological buttons they installed when you were four years old! You react not as a mature adult, but as a wounded child screaming for validation.
> 
> The next time your mother starts to nag, do not react. Take a deep breath. Look at her wrinkled face, her trembling hands, her old eyes. Recognize that she is acting out of her own unconscious fear and conditioning. Feel compassion for her! She knows no better. Hug her, kiss her forehead, bring her a cup of tea. When you stop demanding that your parents approve of you, their power to provoke you vanishes forever."*

### 5. On Artistic and Creative Blocks
> **Questioner:** *"I am a painter, but for the past year my canvas has remained completely blank. I feel dry, barren, and terrified that my creative gift is dead."*
>
> **Osho:** *"Your gift is not dead; your ego has simply gotten in the way!
> 
> You are trying to paint from ambition: you want to paint a masterpiece that will win acclaim, impress critics, and sell for millions. That ambition creates performance anxiety, and anxiety freezes the creative muse!
> 
> Drop the ambition! Do not try to paint a masterpiece. Buy cheap paper and cheap colors. Play like a six-year-old child! Throw colors at the wall, make silly finger paintings, make a mess! The moment you remove the serious adult critic from your shoulder, the creative current will break through the dam and flood your soul with inspiration."*

### 6. On Severe Physical Illness and Chronic Pain
> **Questioner:** *"I am suffering from a chronic, incurable degenerative spinal condition. The pain is constant. How can I meditate when my body is in agony?"*
>
> **Osho:** *"Listen to me with your whole heart: You are NOT the body!
> 
> The body is an outer garment made of biological clay. The nerves report pain: 'There is pain in the lower spine.' The mechanical mind immediately identifies with the message and says: 'I AM IN PAIN!' That is the mistake!
> 
> Separate yourself from the report. Look at the pain as if it is happening to an object five feet away from you. Notice the exact shape of the pain, its burning, its throbbing. Say to yourself: 'The body is in pain; I am the consciousness witnessing the pain.' 
> 
> In that subtle gap between the witness and the sensation, the suffering evaporates. The physical pain remains, but the psychological agony of suffering disappears completely."*

### 7. On the Imminent Death of a Beloved Friend
> **Questioner:** *"My closest friend in the commune is dying of terminal cancer. I sit by her bedside feeling helpless, terrified, and overwhelmed by grief."*
>
> **Osho:** *"Do not sit by her bedside looking like a dark cloud of gloom! If you weep and wail in front of a dying person, you drag them down into panic and heavy earthbound attachments.
> 
> Hold her hand. Sit in absolute, serene silence. Meditate with her! Fill the room with fragrant incense, flowers, and soft, beautiful music. Help her to let go of her body with trust. Tell her: 'Go peacefully into the light; existence is receiving you.'
> 
> Death is a sacred birth into the formless whole. If you truly love her, be a midwife helping her soul to be born into eternity!"*

## Appendix D: The 24-Hour Witnessing Protocol: Integrating *Sakshi* into Modern Urban Life

In his mid-series discourses, Osho provides an operational blueprint for practicing witnessing (*Sakshi*) throughout a conventional 24-hour day without disrupting professional, family, or social obligations:

1. **The Morning Waking Gap (6:00 AM)**: Upon first waking, remain motionless with eyes closed for three minutes. Observe the first thoughts fluttering across the screen of consciousness before the morning agenda begins. Witness the waking transition without immediately latching onto anxieties.
2. **The Mindful Breakfast (7:30 AM)**: Chew each mouthful of food thoroughly. Taste the nuances of flavor, texture, and temperature. Refrain from reading newspapers or checking digital devices while eating; transform biological nourishment into a sacrament of gratitude.
3. **The Transit Witness (8:30 AM)**: While driving or riding public transit, regard every traffic jam or red light as an enlightened teacher commanding: *"Stop! Come home to yourself."* Drop the shoulders, unclench the jaw, and watch the breath rise and fall.
4. **The Midday Occupational Anchor (1:00 PM)**: Every two hours during the workday, take a sixty-second conscious pause. Disengage from screens, look at the sky or a green plant, and silently ask: *"Who is doing this work? Is consciousness undisturbed?"*
5. **The Evening Cathartic Discharge (6:30 PM)**: Before greeting family members, enter a private room for ten minutes. Shake the physical body from feet to crown, exhale forcefully through an open mouth, and discharge the accumulated competitive adrenaline of the marketplace.
6. **The Nighttime Dissolution (10:30 PM)**: Lie flat in Savasana. Systematically release all memories, successes, failures, and grievances from the day. Enter sleep directly from the silent gap between the exhalation and inhalation.
