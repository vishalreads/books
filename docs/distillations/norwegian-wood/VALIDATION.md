## 3. AUDIT OF SPECIFIC RESTORATIONS (V2.5 RECOVERIES)

### 1. Naoko's Elder Sister's Suicide (Content Unit 6.3 & View B Trajectory 1)
- **Source Details Preserved**: Naoko reveals that in sixth grade (age 11), her 17-year-old sister—an exceptionally gifted, beautiful, flawless student—locked her bedroom door and hanged herself from the ceiling beam without warning or note. Naoko unlocked the door and discovered her dangling body. Naoko reflects that ever since, she has felt an unexplained darkness within her family, fearing she was destined for the same silence.
- **Epistemic Hygiene Confirmation**: Preserved strictly as Naoko's personal narrative reflection. The phrase "hereditary family curse" has been completely purged; no deterministic psychiatric or genetic theories are imposed on the source text.
- **Causal Impact**: Restores the vital causal understanding that Naoko's fragility did not begin with Kizuki, but was a deep-seated domestic trauma that preceded his death.

### 2. Toru's Shibuya Record-Store Employment (Content Units 2.4 & 8.1)
- **Source Details Preserved**: Toru works part-time at a record shop in Shibuya specializing in imported jazz and rock vinyl. His wages fund his independent living expenses and later his rent for the small house in Kichijoji.
- **Character & Thematic Impact**: Grounds Toru's economic survival outside institutions and explains his encyclopedic familiarity with Western jazz (Coltrane, Monk, Evans) and rock (The Beatles, Rolling Stones, Cream).

### 3. Ami Hostel's Cashless Barter Economy & Medical Philosophy (Content Unit 6.1 & View B Trajectory 3)
- **Source Details Preserved**: Secluded mountain retreat without locked gates or walls; operates on a cashless barter economy where residents and staff work together in vegetable plots and workshops; doctors and nurses wear ordinary clothes, eat identical communal meals, and openly acknowledge their own vulnerabilities, rejecting hierarchical medical authority.
- **Thematic Impact**: Establishes that Ami Hostel is an egalitarian sanctuary seeking equilibrium rather than clinical conformity.
